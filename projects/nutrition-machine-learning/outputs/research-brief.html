Does diet improve prediction of measured HbA1c?

Matthew Jeans, PhD, PMP | ORCID 0000-0002-1140-3185

Adding diet changed weighted RMSE by -0.0004 HbA1c percentage points (approximate conditional 95% interval -0.0016 to +0.0008). The interval includes zero; this comparison does not establish an improvement.

Abstract

This benchmark asks whether two-day dietary summaries improve prediction of contemporaneously measured HbA1c beyond demographic and body measurements. Models were developed in 3,999 eligible adults from NHANES 2015-2016 and tested in a separate 2017-2018 sample of 3,947 adults. Training-only preprocessing and 4-fold PSU-grouped cross-validation selected Elastic net + diet for the dietary pipeline. Its weighted RMSE was 0.8637, versus 0.8642 for the selected base pipeline. Adding diet changed weighted RMSE by -0.0004 HbA1c percentage points (approximate conditional 95% interval -0.0016 to +0.0008). The interval includes zero; this comparison does not establish an improvement.

Model comparison

What diet adds

Cohort and measurement

Adults aged 20 or older; not known pregnant (unknown retained); two reliable dietary recalls, positive WTDR2D, measured HbA1c, survey-design fields, and positive energy intake. Both predictor sets use identical cohorts. HbA1c is never imputed.

Predictors and training

Base: age, recorded sex, income ratio, BMI, and waist circumference. Diet adds energy and fiber, sugars, saturated fat, protein, and sodium density per 1,000 kcal. Medians, missingness indicators, and scaling are learned within training folds; laboratory outcomes and diabetes-specific variables are excluded from predictors.

Evaluation and uncertainty

WTDR2D weights enter fitting and evaluation. Model selection uses grouped development CV; 1,000 stratified masked-PSU bootstrap resamples quantify approximate conditional test uncertainty. These intervals exclude training/model-selection uncertainty and are not individual prediction intervals.

Limits that affect interpretation

Two recalls do not measure usual intake without error. The samples are independent cross-sections, not longitudinal follow-up. Missing HbA1c and eligibility restrictions can alter representativeness; weighting does not repair every selection process. Diagnosis and treatment may change both diet and HbA1c. Subgroup checks do not establish fairness or safety.

Reproduce and inspect

The source repository provides verified CDC file hashes, pinned dependencies, 19 unit tests, all aggregate tables, the fixed analysis plan, and numeric reference checks. Reproduction reruns the same frozen holdout; it is not a new independent validation study.
